You sold a RM20 item on Shopee. How much actually reaches your bank?

Commission after your vouchers, SST on the fee, a transaction fee on top. Here is how to get Claude to do that maths for every item, with the exact prompt.

By Onyxx Tech28 November 20266 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

Less than RM20, and usually by more than you think. Shopee takes a commission on the price after the vouchers you paid for, charges 8% SST on that commission, and takes a transaction fee as well. The rates have also changed more than once this year. Most sellers never work this out per item, so they price by feel. You do not have to do the maths yourself: paste Shopee's fee notice and your price list into Claude, ask one clear question, and you get a table of what you keep on every product.

Why the number in your head is wrong

When you list a product at RM20, it is natural to think of it as a RM20 sale minus "some fees". The problem is where those fees are calculated from.

Shopee's Marketplace commission is worked out on the final product price after seller-funded discounts, so on the price after the voucher you paid for, not your listing price (BigSeller, Shopee Malaysia commission update). On top of that, all commission fees are subject to 8% SST (Zetpy, Shopee commission fee adjustments, 21 May 2026). Then there is a separate transaction fee for processing the payment.

None of these is huge on its own. Together, on a cheap item with a voucher, they can eat most of your profit.

The rates keep moving

Shopee Malaysia changed its Marketplace commission from 1 February 2026 (BigSeller) and again from 21 May 2026 (Zetpy). Rates also differ by category and by which seller programmes you are in, so there is no single number that is right for every shop.

That is why a sum you did last year is probably out of date. The only rates that count are the ones in your own Seller Centre today.

Why nobody does this by hand

Be honest: when did you last work out, for every product, price minus voucher minus commission minus SST on commission minus transaction fee minus your cost? With 30 products and three voucher levels, that is a spreadsheet afternoon. So most of us guess, and the guess is usually generous.

This is exactly the kind of job Claude is good at. It reads a fee notice in plain language, follows the rules you give it, does the same calculation on every row without getting bored, and shows its working so you can check it.

How to do it with Claude, step by step

You can use the free plan at claude.ai. It takes about ten minutes the first time.

  1. Get the current rates. Open Shopee Seller Centre and find the latest commission and fee notice for your category. Copy the text, or take a screenshot. Claude can read both.
  2. Get your price list. For each product: name, selling price, the voucher or discount you fund, and what the item costs you (stock plus packaging). A copy from your spreadsheet is fine, columns and all.
  3. Open a new chat in Claude. Paste the fee notice first, then your price list.
  4. Paste the prompt below and send.
  5. Check one row yourself with a calculator. If it matches, trust the rest. If it does not, tell Claude which number is wrong and why.

The exact prompt

Copy this as it is. Change nothing except the bits in brackets if you want to.

I sell on Shopee Malaysia. Above is Shopee's current fee notice for my category, and my price list.

For each product, work out: 1. The price after my seller-funded voucher or discount. 2. The commission, calculated on the price after the voucher, using the rate from the notice for my category. 3. 8% SST on the commission. 4. The transaction fee, using the rate in the notice. 5. What I keep: price after voucher, minus commission, minus SST, minus transaction fee. 6. My profit: what I keep, minus my cost. 7. Profit as a percentage of the selling price.

Put it in one table, one row per product, amounts in RM to 2 decimals. Sort it from lowest profit to highest. Under the table, tell me which products make less than [RM2] profit, and which voucher costs me the most. Show the rates you used, and say if anything in the notice was unclear instead of guessing.

The last line matters. If the notice does not say something, you want Claude to ask, not to invent a rate.

What to do with the answer

  • Raise or drop the voucher on the products at the top of the list. A voucher on a low-margin item is often the whole profit.
  • Bundle cheap items with better ones, so the fees are paid once on a bigger order.
  • Reprice anything below your floor. If a product cannot make your minimum after fees, it is advertising, not income. That can be fine, as long as you choose it.
  • Run it again every time Shopee sends a fee notice. Keep the chat, paste the new notice, and ask Claude to redo the table with the new rates.

What not to do

  • Do not use rates from a blog post (including this one). Use the notice in your own Seller Centre.
  • Do not skip checking one row. Claude is good at maths, but it works from what you paste. A wrong rate in gives a wrong table out.
  • Do not paste customer data. Product names and prices are all it needs.

Where to start on Monday

Pick your ten best-selling products. Put the price, your voucher and your cost for each in a list. Open Claude, paste the fee notice and the list, then the prompt. You will have the table before your coffee is cold, and you will probably change at least one voucher that day.

Questions people ask

Do I need the paid version of Claude?

No. The free plan at claude.ai can read the notice and your list and build the table. Paid plans give you more use per day if you run it often.

Can Claude read a screenshot of the fee notice?

Yes. You can paste text or upload a screenshot or PDF. Text is the most reliable, so copy it if you can.

Does this work for Lazada or TikTok Shop?

The method does. Paste that platform's own fee notice and change "Shopee" in the prompt. Each platform calculates its fees differently, which is why the notice matters more than the prompt.

Is Claude's answer always right?

It is only as good as the rates you give it. That is why the prompt asks it to show the rates it used and to flag anything unclear, and why you check one row yourself.
