Are you on Google Maps? Three free steps to get found

When someone nearby searches for what you sell, Google shows a few businesses first. Here is how to become one of them without paying for ads.

By Onyxx Tech25 November 20267 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

Open Google Maps and search for what you sell, the way a customer would: "kedai kek johor bahru", "aircond service Skudai", "klinik gigi near me". If your business is not in the first few results, those calls are going to the shops that are. The fix costs nothing: claim your Google Business Profile, fill in every field, and collect reviews (and answer them). Most owners can do the first two in an afternoon.

Do this test first

Take your phone, open a private browser tab and search three ways:

  1. Your business name.
  2. What you sell plus your area ("bakery Tebrau").
  3. What you sell plus "near me", while standing somewhere your customers would be.

Write down where you appear for each one. That is your starting point.

If you show up for your name but not for the other two, Google knows you exist but does not yet think you are the best answer. If you do not show up even for your name, you probably have no profile at all, or someone else (an old staff member, an ex-partner, a directory) created it and you need to claim it.

How Google decides who shows first

Google does not publish the formula, but it does say what matters. Its own help page names three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches the search. Google says a complete, detailed profile helps here.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching. You cannot change this one.
  • Prominence: how well known you are. Google says this includes how many reviews you have and how many websites link to you.

It also says plainly that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Ads exist, but they are marked as sponsored and sit apart from the normal results.

So two of the three factors are in your hands. The three steps below work on exactly those two.

Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

  1. Go to business.google.com and sign in with a Google account the business owns, not a staff member's personal Gmail. People leave. The account should not leave with them.
  2. Search for your business. If it already exists, claim it. If not, add it.
  3. Use your real name, exactly as on your signboard. No extra keywords (more on that below).
  4. Pick the right primary category ("Bakery", "Car repair shop", "Dental clinic"). Add secondary categories only if they are true.
  5. Set your address or service area. If customers come to you, show the address. If you go to them (aircond, plumbing, catering), set a service area instead.
  6. Verify. Google chooses the method for you: phone or text, email, a recorded video, a live video call, or a postcard (Google: verify your business). For a video, show your signboard, your premises and something that proves you run the business, such as tools, stock or a utility bill. The name in the video must match the profile.

Can't verify? You cannot pick or change the method. If none of the options work, contact Google support from inside your profile rather than creating a second one.

Step 2: Fill in everything

This is the step most businesses skip, and it is the one Google puts numbers on. According to Google's Business Profile help, customers are 70% more likely to visit and 50% more likely to consider buying from a business with a complete profile, and 2.7 times more likely to consider it reputable.

Here is the checklist we use for our clients. Fifteen items, most of which take a minute each:

  1. Business name, exactly as on your sign
  2. Primary and secondary categories
  3. Address or service area
  4. Opening hours, plus special hours for Raya, CNY, Deepavali and Christmas
  5. A phone number that someone answers
  6. Website link, to a page that matches your profile
  7. Chat: a WhatsApp click-to-chat link, if Google offers the Chat option for your profile (Google says chat is only available in some regions)
  8. Description: what you do, for whom, and where
  9. Products and services, with prices if you can
  10. Logo and cover photo
  11. Photos: the outside (so people can find the door), the inside, your team, your products
  12. Attributes: halal, wheelchair access, delivery, card payment and so on
  13. A booking or order link, if you take bookings
  14. A post or offer every month
  15. Reviews, with replies (step 3)

Keep the same details everywhere. Your name, address and phone number should be identical on your profile, your website, Facebook, Instagram and any marketplace you sell on. "No. 12, Jalan Harmonium" in one place and "12 Jln Harmonium" in another is fine. A different phone number or an old address is not. It confuses Google and it confuses customers.

Step 3: Ask for reviews, and reply to every one

Reviews do two jobs. They feed the "prominence" factor, and they are what a customer reads before choosing between you and the shop next door.

When to ask: right after a good moment. The order arrived on time, the job is finished, the customer said thank you. Send your review link (in your profile, look for "Ask for reviews") while they still feel good about it.

Messages you can copy:

English: Thank you for choosing [business]! If you were happy with us, a quick Google review would help us a lot: [link] 🙏

BM: Terima kasih kerana memilih [business]! Kalau puas hati, boleh tinggalkan review Google untuk kami? Sangat membantu: [link] 🙏

How to reply:

  • Good review: thank them by name, mention what they bought, invite them back. One or two sentences is enough.
  • Bad review: stay calm, apologise for the experience, and take it private. Never argue in public. Everyone reading is a future customer, and they are judging the reply more than the complaint.

For example: "Sorry about the late delivery on Saturday, Aina. That is not the standard we aim for. Could you WhatsApp us on [your number]? We would like to put it right."

Do it faster with Claude Code

If writing is the part that slows you down, these prompts help. The first three also work in the normal Claude app. Claude Code is Anthropic's coding assistant, and the fourth prompt needs it because it reads your website files. Check every result before you publish it.

1. Write your description

Write a Google Business Profile description (max 750 characters) for [business], a [type] in [area, Johor Bahru]. We sell [products/services] to [customers]. Plain, friendly English, no keyword stuffing, no prices that may change. Then give me a Bahasa Melayu version.

2. List your services

Turn this price list into Google Business Profile services, each with a short description: [paste]. Group them into 3 to 5 sections.

3. Reply to reviews

Draft short, warm replies to these Google reviews for [business]. For negative ones, apologise, don't argue, and invite them to contact us at [phone]. Keep each under 60 words: [paste reviews].

4. Check your website matches

Read my website code in this folder. Check that the business name, address, phone and opening hours match exactly: [paste from your profile]. Add LocalBusiness structured data (JSON-LD) with these details, and list anything that doesn't match.

What not to do

  • Do not stuff keywords into your name. "Best Cake Shop JB Murah Halal" is tempting. Google's guidelines say adding extra information to your business name is not permitted and can get your profile suspended. Put keywords in the description and services instead.
  • Do not buy reviews, review yourself, or offer a discount for reviews. Google removes fake and incentivised reviews, and it is not worth the risk to your profile.
  • Do not create a second profile for the same location because the first one is stuck. Google allows one profile per business location, and duplicates cause problems with how you show up.
  • Do not let one staff member own the profile on their personal account. Add them as a manager instead.

Where to start on Monday

  • Twenty minutes: do the three-search test above, then claim or create your profile and start verification.
  • One hour, once verified: work through the 15-point checklist. Photos first: they take the longest to gather and make the biggest difference to how the profile looks.
  • This week: send the review message to five happy customers. Reply to every review that comes in, the good and the bad.

Then search again in a month and compare with what you wrote down.

Questions people ask

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing the profile costs nothing. You only pay if you choose to run Google Ads, which is separate.

I work from home or go to my customers. Can I still have one?

Yes. Set a service area and hide your home address. Google has rules for service-area businesses, so you appear for the areas you serve without showing where you live.

Do I still need a website if I have a profile?

Your profile is where people find you. Your website is where they decide. Google lists links from other websites as part of "prominence", and AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Gemini build their answers from what websites say. A profile with no website behind it is a shop with no signboard inside.

How long until I move up?

Google does not give a timeline, and anyone who promises "page one in two weeks" is guessing. A complete profile and a steady flow of reviews is what Google itself says to work on.
