Customers order on WhatsApp and never collect? Make them pay first

How a simple pre-order system collects the money before you cook, counts the orders for you, and ends the chasing.

By Onyxx Tech28 November 20266 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

If customers order in your WhatsApp group and pay only when they collect, some of them will not come, and the food you cooked for them is lost. The fix is to flip the order of things: payment first, then the order counts. A pre-order system does this for you. It is your own order page where customers choose their food and a pickup time and pay online by FPX or DuitNow QR, and at your cut-off time it gives you the full list of paid orders, so you cook exactly what was paid for.

Why do customers order and never collect?

Because ordering costs them nothing. A message in a group takes two seconds. If plans change, they simply do not come, and nothing happens to them. Everything happens to you: the rice is cooked, the sambal is made, the packs are wrapped.

Most sellers know the lines by heart. "On the way." "Nanti I transfer." "Can I collect tomorrow?" None of them are bad people. They just have not paid, so the order does not feel real to them yet.

Online sellers feel it too. This month The Star reported that complaints about cash-on-delivery orders more than doubled: 46 in the first eight months of 2026, against 18 for the whole of 2025, and the government is drafting an E-Commerce Bill so buyers must pay for goods they ordered and received (The Star, 10 November 2026). That law is about parcels, not your nasi lemak group. But the lesson is the same: when payment comes last, the seller carries the risk.

Why "just ask them to transfer first" is not enough

The obvious answer is to ask everyone to transfer before you cook. It works, partly. Now you have a new job: matching screenshots to names, late at night, one by one, and scrolling back through the group to find who ordered what.

Screenshots are also easy to fake. In 2022 a cake shop in Sabah received a "receipt" showing RM590 for an RM135 order, with a request to refund the difference. The owner spotted that it was edited (World of Buzz, 6 April 2022). A screenshot proves nothing. Money arriving in your account does.

What is a pre-order system?

A pre-order system is your own order page where customers order and pay first. It collects the money, then counts the orders for you. Customers do not download anything. They open a link.

It does three things a WhatsApp group cannot do:

  1. It takes the payment at the moment of ordering.
  2. It checks every payment automatically, with the payment provider, not with a screenshot.
  3. It closes itself at your cut-off time and hands you the totals.

How does it work, step by step?

One. You share your order page link in your WhatsApp group. Same group, same customers. You post the link instead of collecting names.

Two. Customers pick their food and a pickup time, and pay online. The page shows today's menu and how many of each item are left. They choose, pick a slot (say 12:00 to 12:30), and pay by FPX online banking or DuitNow QR. The payment goes through a Malaysian payment gateway, such as toyyibPay, which supports FPX and DuitNow QR (toyyibPay plugin page). Gateways charge a small fee per payment, so check the current rates before you choose one.

Three. At cut-off, orders close and you get the full list on your phone. The gateway tells the system each payment went through, and only then does the order appear on your list: name, food, pickup time. When your cut-off time arrives, the page stops taking orders by itself. You see, for example, thirty paid nasi lemak. You cook thirty.

At pickup you tick each name off. If someone paid and still does not come, the food is already paid for.

What it fixes

  • No cooking for people who never come. Every order on your list is paid.
  • No chasing transfers. Nobody to remind, nothing to match.
  • No guessing how much to cook. The total is on your phone at cut-off.
  • No fake receipts. You never rely on a screenshot.

What you can do this week, even without a system

You can start the habit before anything is built:

  1. Announce one rule in your group: "From Monday, orders are confirmed only after payment."
  2. Set a cut-off time and stick to it. "Orders close 10 pm for tomorrow's lunch."
  3. Only count paid orders when you plan your cooking. If it is not paid, it is not an order.
  4. Check the money in your bank app, not the screenshot, before you mark someone as paid.
  5. Write down your pickup policy (for example, uncollected paid orders are not refunded after pickup time) and pin it in the group.

This works with a small group. Once you have more than a few dozen orders a day, checking each payment by hand is the part that breaks, and that is where a pre-order system earns its keep.

What not to do

  • Do not move your customers to an app. Most of them live in WhatsApp. Keep the link in the group they already use.
  • Do not take orders in two places. If you accept "+1" messages as well as the link, you are back to counting by hand.
  • Do not skip the cut-off. Late orders after you have bought ingredients are how waste comes back.

Questions people ask

Do my customers need to download an app?

No. They tap a link in your WhatsApp group, order and pay in their phone's browser.

How do I know a customer really paid?

The payment gateway confirms each payment to the system directly. An order only appears on your list after that confirmation, so you never need a screenshot.

Does the money go to my own bank account?

Yes. The gateway collects the payment and pays it out to your registered bank account. How quickly depends on the gateway, so check its payout schedule.

What if a customer pays but does not collect?

You have already been paid. Set a clear pickup policy and pin it in your group so customers know what happens to uncollected orders.
