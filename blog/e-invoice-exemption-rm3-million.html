Under RM3 million in sales? You may not need e-invoices

LHDN raised the e-invoice exemption from RM1 million to RM3 million. Here is who it covers, the catch for companies in a group, and what to do if you are not exempt.

By Onyxx Tech26 November 20266 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

If your business makes less than RM3 million a year, you may not have to issue e-invoices at all. LHDN raised the exemption threshold from RM1 million to RM3 million, and there is nothing to apply for. But the exemption has conditions, and businesses that are part of a group are the ones most often caught out. This post explains who is exempt, who is not, and what to check today. It is general information, not tax advice: confirm your own position with your accountant or tax agent.

What changed

Under LHDN's e-Invoice Guideline (Version 4.8), taxpayers with an annual turnover or revenue below RM3,000,000 are currently exempt from issuing e-invoices. Before this, the threshold was RM1 million. LHDN's e-Invoice FAQ says the change followed the Prime Minister's announcement, and that no application or approval is needed to use the exemption.

Are you exempt? Check these three things

1. Your annual turnover or revenue is below RM3 million. Look at last year's figure, not this month's sales.

2. You are not linked to a bigger business. The exemption does not apply if your business has a holding company, a related company, a joint venture partner or a non-individual shareholder whose turnover is RM3 million or more. A small company owned by a large group still has to follow the normal timeline.

3. You have not already crossed RM3 million. Once your turnover reaches RM3 million, the FAQ explains when you must start, and the exemption does not come back just because sales fall later. Check the FAQ examples for the year that applies to you.

If all three are true, you are currently exempt.

If you are exempt

  • You do not have to issue e-invoices, and you do not need to register for anything to claim the exemption.
  • Keep your normal invoices and receipts as before.
  • Watch your turnover. If you are growing towards RM3 million, plan your system before you cross the line, not after.

If you are not exempt

You need a way to issue e-invoices that LHDN validates. There are three routes:

  1. MyInvois Portal: LHDN's free portal, fine for a small number of invoices typed in by hand.
  2. Your accounting or POS software: many already support e-invoicing. Ask your provider.
  3. A connection from your own system: if your orders and invoices already live in a custom system, it can send them to LHDN automatically, so nobody retypes anything.

Check today

  • Find last year's turnover figure.
  • List any holding, related or joint-venture companies and their turnover.
  • If you are close to RM3 million, talk to your accountant now.

Questions people ask

Do I need to apply for the exemption?

No. The FAQ says no separate application or approval from LHDN is needed.

My sales are RM2.5 million, but my parent company makes RM10 million. Am I exempt?

No. The exemption does not apply where a holding company, related company or other non-individual shareholder has turnover of RM3 million or more.

Can I still issue e-invoices if I am exempt?

Some businesses choose to, for example when their larger customers ask for them. Check with your accountant whether that makes sense for you.

Where do I find the official rules?

LHDN's e-Invoice pages at hasil.gov.my, especially the General Guideline and the FAQ. Always check the latest version: the threshold has changed before.
