How much does a website cost in Malaysia? RM800 vs RM20,000

Two quotes for the same website can be twenty times apart, and both can be fair. Here is what you are paying for, the costs that are not in the quote, and what to ask before you pay.

By Onyxx Tech25 November 20268 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

A website in Malaysia can cost anywhere from about RM1,000 to well over RM25,000, because "a website" covers three different products: a template site, a custom business site and a system. Two quotes for the same brief can be far apart and both be fair. Before you compare prices, find out which of the three you are being quoted for, and what each will do for your business.

Why one quote says RM800 and another says RM20,000

Say you ask two companies for "a website". One sends RM800, the other RM20,000. The cheap one is probably offering a ready-made template with your logo and a few pages. The expensive one is probably offering a site designed around your customers, written to be found on Google, with enquiry forms and tracking, and possibly some system work behind it.

Neither is lying. They are selling different things under the same word. The mistake is comparing the two numbers without comparing what is inside them.

The three tiers

All prices below are published by Malaysian web agencies themselves in 2026. They are not our prices, and every agency prices differently.

1. Template site: usually a few thousand ringgit

A ready-made design with your logo, photos and text, and a handful of pages. Rebrand lists template-based sites at RM1,000 to RM3,000 and simple brochure sites at RM2,000 to RM5,000. Kangxiang publishes a one-page template site at RM1,980 and a 6-page template site at RM3,280.

Good for: a simple online presence, fast. Watch for: it looks like many other sites, and whether it shows up on Google depends entirely on who sets it up.

2. Custom business site: often RM5,000 to RM25,000

Designed around your business and your customers, structured to be found on Google, with enquiry forms, WhatsApp buttons and tracking. Walk Production's 2026 planning bands put a 10 to 15 page corporate site at RM5,000 to RM25,000, depending on how much is custom, written for you and optimised.

Good for: a business that wants enquiries from its website, not just a page that exists.

3. A system: depends on what it has to do

Online orders, stock, bookings, customer portals, dashboards for your team. The price follows the work: how many screens, how many users and roles, and what it connects to (payments, e-invoicing, accounting). Rebrand lists custom web apps from RM10,000 to RM25,000 and up, and Walk Production puts e-commerce builds anywhere from RM6,000 to RM60,000. Large custom builds go much higher.

Good for: replacing manual work, such as retyping WhatsApp orders into Excel.

The costs that are not in the quote

The build price is only the first year. Ask about each of these before you sign:

  • Domain name, renewed every year. Rebrand puts it at RM10 to RM30 a year. Make sure it is registered in your name, not the developer's.
  • Hosting, monthly or yearly. Rebrand lists RM30 to RM200 or more a month depending on traffic.
  • Maintenance: updates, security fixes and backups. Published figures range from RM300 to RM2,000 a year (Rebrand) to RM6,000 to RM12,000 a year for a managed WordPress site (Walk Production).
  • Content: copywriting, photos and product listings are often charged separately.
  • Changes after launch: what does a new page or a text change cost?
  • Integrations: payment gateway fees, WhatsApp, accounting, and e-invoicing if your sales are above LHDN's current threshold (check hasil.gov.my).
  • Email accounts on your own domain.
  • Google setup: is search optimisation and your Google Business Profile included, or extra?

A useful habit from Jumix's own pricing guide: add up three years for every quote (build, hosting, maintenance, content). A cheap build with an expensive yearly bill catches up quickly.

Why the cheapest quote can cost more

A cheap site is not a bad site. But when it is cheap because corners were cut, you pay later:

  • Slow pages on a phone. Visitors leave before it loads.
  • Nothing set up for Google. Nobody finds the site, so it brings no enquiries.
  • It cannot grow. When you need online orders or a booking system, it gets rebuilt from scratch, and you pay twice.

So the first question is not "how much?". It is "what will this website do for my business?"

10 questions to ask before you pay a deposit

  1. Is the price fixed, and exactly what is included (pages, features, revisions)?
  2. Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the code when we are done?
  3. Can I see three live sites you built, and talk to one of those clients?
  4. How will it be found on Google? What search optimisation is included?
  5. How fast will it load on a phone, and how do you test that?
  6. Can I update text and photos myself?
  7. What does maintenance cost per month, and what happens if I stop paying?
  8. Who do I talk to during the project, and how fast do you reply?
  9. What is the timeline, and what do you need from me?
  10. What will this website do for my business: more enquiries, fewer manual tasks, more sales?

Walk away if

  • The domain is registered in their name. You cannot leave without them.
  • There are no live examples. Only screenshots, or sites that no longer exist.
  • They guarantee "number one on Google". Nobody can promise a ranking.
  • The quote is vague. No list of pages or features means every change becomes an extra charge.

Compare quotes with Claude

These prompts help you see the difference between quotes without taking anyone's word for it. The first works in the normal Claude app. The second works best in Claude Code, Anthropic's coding assistant, which can open and check a live website.

1. Put two quotes side by side

Here are two website quotes for my [type of business] in [city]: [paste both]. Put them side by side: what's included, what's missing, who owns the domain and code, ongoing costs, and the questions I should ask each one. Don't recommend a vendor; just make the differences clear.

2. Check a developer's past work

Check this website: [URL]. Is it mobile-friendly, does it have a clear title and description for Google, are the business name, address and phone easy to find, and is there an obvious way to enquire? List the 5 biggest problems in plain language.

Where to start on Monday

  • Decide which tier you need. Write down what the website must do for your business in one sentence.
  • Ask for itemised quotes from two or three developers, using the 10 questions above.
  • Add up three years for each quote, not just the build price.

Questions people ask

Why are freelancers cheaper than agencies?

Lower overheads, and usually a smaller scope. Walk Production's guide lists a basic 1 to 5 page site at RM1,000 to RM4,000 from a freelancer and RM3,000 to RM8,000 from an agency. A good freelancer can be great value. Check the same things you would check with an agency: live examples, ownership and who maintains it.

Is a website builder like Wix or Shopify enough?

For a simple presence or a small shop, often yes. You pay a monthly subscription instead of a big build fee. Check what you can and cannot change, and what happens to your site if you stop paying.
