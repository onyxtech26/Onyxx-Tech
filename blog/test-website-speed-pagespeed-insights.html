Is your website slow? Check it in 10 seconds, then fix it

Google's free PageSpeed Insights grades your website in seconds. How to read the score, and the fixes that make the biggest difference for small business sites.

By Onyxx Tech26 November 20266 min read

Download as PDFFree. Save it, print it, share it with your team.

Most people visit your website on a phone, often on mobile data. If it takes too long to load, they leave before they see what you sell. You can check your own site in about ten seconds with Google's free tool, PageSpeed Insights, and most of the fixes are simpler than people expect.

How to test

  1. Go to pagespeed.web.dev.
  2. Paste your website address and press Analyze.
  3. Look at the Mobile tab first.

How to read the score

The big number at the top is the performance score, from 0 to 100. According to Google:

  • 90 or more: good
  • 50 to 89: needs improvement
  • under 50: poor

The mobile test is deliberately tough: Google slows the network and processor down to imitate a typical phone, so mobile scores are usually lower than desktop.

If your site has enough visitors, you will also see real-user data from the last 28 days, with the Core Web Vitals marked as passed or failed. That is how real visitors experienced your site.

The fixes, in order

1. Shrink your photos. A photo straight from a phone can be several megabytes. Resize it to the size it is shown at, and save it as WebP. In our projects, this is usually the single biggest improvement.

2. Remove what you do not use. Unused plugins, sliders, chat widgets and tracking scripts all add weight. If nobody would miss it, remove it.

3. Use good hosting. Cheap shared hosting can be slow before your site even starts loading. Hosting close to your customers helps.

4. Load less at the top of the page. Big videos and sliders at the top delay everything. One strong image and a clear message load faster and work better.

5. Test again. Run PageSpeed Insights after each change, on the same page, so you know what helped.

Questions people ask

Does speed affect Google rankings?

Page experience is one of many things Google looks at, but the bigger reason is customers: a slow site loses them before they read anything.

My score changes every time I test. Why?

Lab tests vary a little each run. Look at the trend over a few tests, and at the real-user data if you have it.

Is 100 necessary?

No. Aim for 90 or above on mobile for your key pages, and fix anything in the red.
