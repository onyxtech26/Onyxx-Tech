AI agent, chatbot or automation: which one do you need?

These three words get used interchangeably by people selling them. They solve different problems, and picking the wrong one is how budgets get wasted.

By Onyxx Tech15 September 20265 min read

A useful way to tell them apart is to ask what starts the work, and who decides what happens next.

Automation: fixed trigger, fixed steps

Something happens, and a predetermined sequence runs. A form is submitted, so a row is added to a sheet, an email goes out and a task appears in your project tool. No judgement is involved. Nothing decides anything.

This is the oldest and least glamorous of the three, and for most businesses it is where the biggest, cheapest wins are. If a person is doing the same eight keystrokes every morning, you do not need AI. You need a workflow.

Chatbot: a person starts it, and it answers

A customer sends a message and gets a reply. The bot's job is to understand the question and respond usefully, which may include collecting details or handing over to a human.

The defining constraint is that a chatbot is reactive. It waits. It is measured on how well it responds, not on what it accomplishes. That makes it perfect for enquiries, opening hours, order status and qualifying leads. It is a poor fit for anything that has to happen whether or not a customer asks.

AI agent: given a goal, it works out the steps

An agent is handed an objective rather than a script. It decides what to do, uses tools to do it, checks the result, and keeps going until the objective is met or it gets stuck and asks for help.

That difference, deciding the steps rather than following them, is the important part. It is also why agents are harder to build well and need guardrails that automations do not.

This is no longer experimental. Industry surveys through 2026 put the share of companies using agents in some form at around 79%, with roughly half having them in production rather than in a pilot. The common production uses are unglamorous: ticket resolution, invoice matching, document processing, lead follow-up.

A quick way to choose

If the problem sounds like…You want…
"The same thing has to happen every time X occurs"Automation
"Customers keep asking us the same things"Chatbot
"Someone has to look at each one and decide"AI agent
"We need all three eventually"Start with the automation

The mistake worth avoiding

Reaching for an agent when an automation would do. Agents cost more to build, cost more to run, and introduce a category of failure that fixed workflows do not have: they can be confidently wrong.

Where the rule is fixed, encode the rule. Save the model for where the input is messy and a human would otherwise have to read it. The best systems we build are usually mostly plumbing with a small amount of intelligence at the one point that needs it.
