AI Agents

AI agents that do the actual work.

Most “AI” in business is a chat window someone has to remember to open. An agent is different: it holds a goal, uses your tools, and finishes the job without being babysat.

#01

An agent is only worth building if it can finish something.

A summariser that hands you a paragraph to re-check has moved the work, not removed it. We scope agents around a task with a definite end — a quote drafted, a record updated, a report filed — so you can tell whether it worked.

#02

It has to live where the work already lives.

Your team is not going to move into a new dashboard. We connect agents to the things you already use — WhatsApp, email, spreadsheets, your database, your CRM — so the output lands where someone was already looking.

#03

Start with one narrow job, not a platform.

The fastest way to waste a budget is to design an all-knowing assistant. We pick the single most repetitive decision in your week, automate that, and only widen the scope once it has earned the trust.

What you get
  • A scoped agent with a defined task and a measurable finish line
  • Integration with the tools you already run on
  • Human approval steps wherever the agent touches money or customers
  • A log of every action it takes, so you can audit what it did
  • Model choice matched to the job — not the most expensive one by default
  • Handover documentation and a walkthrough with your team

Common questions

A chatbot answers. An agent acts. A chatbot can tell a customer your opening hours; an agent can check your calendar, book the slot, write it to your system and send the confirmation. If the job ends with something changing in your business, you want an agent.

A first working agent on a narrow task is usually two to four weeks from the kickoff conversation. Broader multi-step agents that touch several systems take longer, mostly because of access and approvals on your side rather than the build itself.

Language models can. That is exactly why we scope agents to tasks with checkable outputs and put an approval step in front of anything that spends money, contacts a customer or edits a record. The agent proposes; a person confirms until it has proven itself.
